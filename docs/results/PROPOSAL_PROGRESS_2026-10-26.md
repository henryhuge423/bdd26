This table is an exploratory chronology of hypotheses that share audit data. It is not an unbiased hyperparameter ranking, an aggregate significance test, or proof that an entire method family cannot work. A positive point estimate in one seed, or a positive seed-specific confidence interval, does not override the pre-existing conjunction of absolute recall, gain and confidence interval, precision, overall coverage, support, and nondegeneracy requirements.

Three failure profiles are visible. First, the original controls and P13 fit the training images extremely well, but audit reliability remains low. In P13, fit internal-Dead recall of .998476/1 accompanies audit recall of .351724/.358621. Extra rare-positive exposure alone did not transfer. Second, restricted adaptation (P10), dropout (P12), and smaller encoder updates (P14) do not provide a reliable generalization benefit; reducing effective adaptation is not automatically better. Third, P15 substantially reduces recall even on unaugmented fit images: overall recall falls from about .978 to .470, and internal-Dead recall from about .952 to .45. This differs from high-fit/low-audit overfitting and is consistent with difficulty learning the tested augmented task within the fixed budget. None of these profiles identifies a causal bottleneck on its own.

P15 shows why precision must be interpreted together with coverage. Audit precision rises from .692/.698 to .865/.863, while candidate counts fall from 10469/10428 to 5498/5397 and unique matches fall from 7241/7280 to 4758/4657. Overall audit recall falls by 20.27/21.41 percentage points; internal-Dead recall falls by 19.31/8.97 points, and both original 95% confidence intervals are below 0. The unchanged 512-candidate cap never activates. White borders, partial nuclei, removal of approximately 9.94% of training center targets per epoch, and stronger invariance demands all change together in this recipe. Do not attribute the failure solely to position embeddings or conclude that spatial augmentation in general is harmful. See the [P15 full closeout](PROPOSAL_P15_RESULTS_2026-10-26.md).

The supported audit sample remains 11 Dead-positive images with 145 internal-Dead objects; Lung and Colon contain 116/145 objects. Tissue-image bootstrap intervals describe uncertainty conditional on this reused image collection. They do not provide independent-patient evidence, protect against sequential hypothesis selection, or quantify random-seed population uncertainty. Small tissue cells cannot support tissue-specific rescue claims or new selection rules. No method in this proposal sequence has earned expansion to masks or the original Phase B.

P16 tests a previously untested objective in this sequence: direct squared error to the existing Gaussian heatmap. C retains the original exact-center focal supervision. R minimizes coefficient 1 FP32 summed squared error between sigmoid outputs and Gaussian targets, divided by the full effective-batch object count. In the original focal objective, the soft Gaussian halo suppresses nearby negative loss; in R, below-target halo outputs receive an attractive gradient. P6 changed only the negative-suppression width, while P11 redistributed exact-center positive mass. P16 is therefore a distinct objective test. Because it also changes total loss scale and background weighting, an eventual result cannot identify halo supervision alone as the cause or establish calibration.

P16 keeps the existing architecture and initialization, last 12 trainable encoder blocks, encoder LR 1e-5, head LR 3e-4, original full fit permutation, D4/color augmentation, 130 epochs, BF16, last-checkpoint evaluation, unaugmented fit/audit evaluation, decoding, and original admission. C/R × seeds 19/1 are four fresh runs, each with its own control. There is no P15 translation, loss-scale/sigma/threshold menu, extra seed, or automatic confirmation. The original F1=C/T1=R gate must pass in both seeds. Failure or an inconclusive result closes the fixed coefficient-1/sigma-1 squared-error recipe. This is a controlled exploratory experiment selected under the user's instruction to proceed to the next Expanse submission, not evidence of expected benefit.
